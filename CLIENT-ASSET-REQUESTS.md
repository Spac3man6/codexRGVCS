# Client Asset Request List

Use this as the short request list for the client.

## Loom-Derived Priority

The homepage hero is the most important missing asset.

- Typography, contrast, and color choices depend heavily on the hero image
- Drone-style hero video is a risky fallback because performance will likely suffer, especially on mobile
- Launch speed matters, but weak media will hurt trust and conversion before testing even starts
- If the client cannot supply a strong real hero image quickly, AI can be used temporarily, but it is still a downgrade

## Highest Priority Items

1. One real wide-format hero photo
   - Best option: a finished project with strong composition, natural light, and enough width for desktop hero use
2. Two to four real concrete staining photos
   - We are currently weakest on true staining examples
3. Two before-and-after pairs
   - These are high-value proof assets for the homepage, gallery, and case studies
4. Two team or process photos
   - Owner portrait, crew on site, active grinding/prep/install, or detail shots of work in progress
5. Two residential outdoor photos
   - Patios, porches, poolside, or outdoor living areas with finished concrete surfaces

## Helpful Secondary Items

1. One or two garage photos with full context
   - Wide enough to show the whole space, not just floor detail
2. One or two commercial wide shots
   - Retail, office, showroom, hospitality, or warehouse environments
3. Any client-approved project facts
   - City, surface type, approximate size, finish system used, and key challenge solved

## Note On AI Assets

We can use AI-generated images or video as temporary placeholders when real project assets are missing, but the quality of proof is lower.

- AI can help keep momentum and fill layout gaps
- AI is weaker for trust, authenticity, and case-study credibility
- Real project photography should replace AI in the homepage hero, gallery proof, team/story sections, and case studies as soon as possible

## Best Client-Friendly Framing

If the client is slow to send assets, ask for:

- one best hero photo
- one best staining photo
- one best before-and-after pair
- one best team or crew photo

That smaller ask usually gets a response faster than requesting everything at once.

## Stronger Client Message

Use this if the verbal ask felt too loose:

> We need to launch quickly, and the most important missing item is one strong hero image. The homepage design, text contrast, and overall feel depend on that photo. If you can send only a few things in the next 24 hours, please prioritize: 1) one wide hero photo, 2) one real staining photo, 3) one before-and-after pair, and 4) one team or crew photo. If you truly do not have a usable hero image, we can use AI temporarily, but the quality, trust, and performance will be lower, especially on mobile.

## What The Off-The-Cuff Message Left Implicit

If you want the client ask to land harder next time, make these points explicit:

1. Exact priority order
   - The original message made the hero sound important, but it did not reduce the ask to a clear top-four list until late
2. What qualifies as a usable hero
   - Say wide, high-resolution, real project photo, strong composition, room for text overlay, and not heavily cluttered
3. Why drone video is not the preferred solution
   - Call out mobile performance and conversion risk directly
4. What happens if they send nothing
   - State that AI fallback is possible, but trust and quality will drop
5. Delivery urgency in practical terms
   - Ask for whatever they can send within 24 hours, even if incomplete, instead of waiting for a full asset package
