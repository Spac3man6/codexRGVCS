## RGV Concrete Stain, September 2, 2026

This is a plain-English summary of a full review of your website's search performance. It covers what we found, what it means for your phone ringing, and what we are doing about it.

## The headline: your customers are searching for epoxy, not staining

We pulled the search data from your own Google Business Profile for April through September. Here are the search terms Google reports:

| What people searched | Times |
|---|---|
| epoxy floor | 134 |
| epoxy garage floor | 93 |
| concrete stain | 72 |
| epoxy flooring | 66 |
| garage floor epoxy | 57 |
| epoxy floor coating | 38 |

**Every term above the reporting cutoff except one contains the word "epoxy."**

An important caveat, because we would rather be accurate than dramatic. Google only shows search terms that cross a minimum threshold, and hides everything below it. There are roughly 40 more terms Google is not showing us. So we can say confidently that epoxy phrasing dominates the terms we can see. We cannot yet say what share of your total search demand it represents, and we are not going to put a percentage in front of you that we cannot defend.

What we can act on right now is the phrasing, which does not depend on the missing data: **people type "epoxy garage floor," not "garage floor coatings."** Your garage page uses the word "garage" 37 times and the word "epoxy" twice. Your epoxy page uses "epoxy" 25 times and "garage" twice. The two highest-value phrases need both words together, and neither page delivers that.

Your website leads with concrete staining, and epoxy sat third in the menu with garage work last. That ordering is worth revisiting, but we want the fuller demand picture first. Before recommending anything that changes your positioning, we will pull your Search Console data and independent keyword volumes for the McAllen area. That is about 45 minutes of work and it costs nothing.

None of this is a criticism of the business name.

## The most urgent problem: old pages that still rank are broken

Your previous website had a large set of pages, one for each service in each city. Things like garage flooring in Pharr, garage flooring in Harlingen, epoxy flake flooring, and a color chart.

When the site was rebuilt, those pages went away. **Google still has them listed and is still showing them to people searching right now. Every one of them leads to an error page.**

We have confirmed 17 of these so far, and expect more once we pull the full list from Search Console. Two of them are currently the *first* result for exactly the kind of commercial job you want. Meanwhile, none of your five new city pages showed up in any of the searches we ran.

In plain terms: Google is actively sending people looking for your services to dead pages.

The fix is to redirect each old address to the closest page on the new site, so that traffic lands somewhere useful instead of an error page.

Two honest notes on it. First, we need to pull the complete list from your Search Console before building the redirects, because the ones we found by searching are a sample and there are almost certainly more. Second, a redirect only recovers the old page's search standing when it points somewhere genuinely equivalent. Several of these old pages were city-specific garage flooring pages, and where you have a matching city page we will point them there. Where you do not, the redirect mostly stops people landing on an error, which is still worth doing but is a smaller win.

Budget half a day for the list and the redirects together. **This is still the single highest-value item on this list and we recommend starting it immediately.**

## Your Spanish pages are a genuine, unusual advantage

We searched in Spanish for concrete and epoxy work across the Valley. The results came back almost entirely empty of local contractors: Wikipedia, YouTube, and companies in Monterrey, Mexico City, and Peru.

We then checked the websites of five competitors in your market. **Not one of the five had a Spanish version of their site.** That is a sample, not a census, so we would not claim you are the only one in the Valley. But it is a strong signal.

In a market as heavily Hispanic as the Rio Grande Valley, that looks like an open lane, and you already have Spanish pages built while none of the competitors we checked do. As of this week those pages also have Spanish menus, Spanish forms, and a visible way for Spanish speakers to find them.

Before we build more Spanish pages, two things need checking, and one of them is a question for you.

First, an empty search result can mean untapped demand, or it can mean nobody searches that way. We want independent keyword volumes for Spanish terms in the McAllen area before committing more hours.

Second, and this is where your ear matters more than our research: your existing Spanish pages use "tinción" for staining and "garaje" for garage. Those are correct Spanish, but the words people actually type in the Valley may be "tinte" or "concreto teñido" and "cochera." If the pages target the wrong words, an empty search result stays empty. Vic will make that call as a native speaker before we build anything further.

## Where you are losing customers to details

These are listed in order of how much they actually matter. That order is deliberate, and it is not the order most agencies would give you.

**1. Google only knows you serve "Pharr and nearby areas." This is the big one.** Your website lists seven cities and you have dedicated pages for five of them. Your Google profile names none of them. This is a single field inside your own Google profile, and fields inside Google's own profile are by far the heaviest factor in whether you appear in local results. It is a ten-minute edit. **If you only do one thing from this entire document, do this one.**

**2. Your hours do not match anywhere.** Google says you close at 6:00. Your website says 5:00. Yelp says 8:30 to 5:00. Google uses hours to decide whether to show you to someone searching right now, so at minimum Google and your website need to agree. Tell us which is right and we will align them.

**3. Your Yelp listing is unclaimed and shows 3.3 stars.** We checked it directly. Nobody has ever claimed it, it displays 3.3 stars from 3 reviews, and it lists a phone number that is not the one on your website or your Google profile.

That 3.3 is the problem, not the phone number. You are 4.5 stars from 51 reviews on Google. Someone comparing contractors who lands on Yelp sees 3.3 and moves on. Claiming the listing lets you fix the number, fix the hours, and respond to those three reviews.

There is also a straightforward money question: Yelp shows a call button with that other number on it. If that line is dead, everyone who taps it reaches nobody.

**4. Two directories list an old address.** Your Better Business Bureau and Procore listings both show an address in Alamo rather than Pharr, dating back to 2016. Worth correcting when you have a spare hour. Honestly, this one is close to cosmetic and we would not want you to prioritize it.

**5. Your 51 reviews averaging 4.5 stars are invisible on your website.** Someone who arrives from anywhere other than Google sees no reviews at all.

**A note on why the order above is what it is.** There is a common belief in the SEO industry that having your name, address, and phone number match perfectly across every directory on the internet is critical for ranking. That was true roughly ten years ago. Google has since gotten much better at recognizing that listings refer to the same business even when details differ. In current published research on local ranking factors, directory listings rank sixth out of seven categories in importance, well behind your Google profile, your reviews, and your website itself.

We are telling you this because we would rather point you at the things that work than bill you for tidying directories.

**One exception worth knowing about.** Those same directory listings matter roughly twice as much when someone asks ChatGPT or Google's AI for a contractor recommendation, because that is what those systems read. That is a growing share of how people find local businesses, and it is the real reason we still want Yelp and BBB cleaned up.

## What we need from you

Some of the highest-value work is blocked until you answer a few questions. We will not put anything on your website that we cannot back up, so we need these in writing.

1. **Insurance.** Your Instagram says "insured and licensed." Do you carry general liability insurance, and can you send us a current certificate?
2. **Licensing.** What does "licensed" refer to? A city or county business license, a manufacturer certification, or something else? Which body issued it?
3. **Warranty.** One of your replies to a Google review mentions standing by your warranty. Do you offer a written one? If so, please send the actual document: what it covers, for how long, and under what conditions.
4. **Polyaspartic and other systems.** Your other listings mention polyaspartic one-day flooring, grind and seal, and urethane mortar. Your website does not mention any of them. Which of these do you currently offer? These are terms customers search for and we would like to use them.
5. **How long you have been in business.** Your website says over two decades. Other listings show 2009. Which is right?

**Why this matters both ways.** If these credentials are real, your website is currently under-selling you: commercial customers and builders routinely check for insurance before hiring. If any of them are not something you can document, we should not be publishing them, and you may want to adjust where they already appear. Either way we need to know before we write anything.

## What we are not recommending

For transparency, a few things we deliberately decided against:

- **Adding star ratings to your website's code.** Google's rules do not allow a business to publish its own review scores this way, and it can trigger a penalty. We will link to your real Google reviews instead.
- **Online booking.** Your competitors' profiles offer it, but this trade cannot quote a floor without seeing it. It would fill your calendar with unqualified appointments.
- **Copying competitors' licensing and insurance claims.** One competitor advertises these prominently. We will not match that until you send us documentation, for the reasons above.
- **Embedding a Google map.** It would slow every page down for very little benefit. Your address is already published in a form Google reads.
