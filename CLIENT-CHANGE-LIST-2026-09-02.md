# Website Change List, September 2, 2026

**Status: all changes below are built and tested, waiting to go live on the website.**

Two items, the home page headline and the Spanish wording throughout, are still with us for a final language review before they publish. They are marked below.

---

## 1. Service names in the footer were showing in all lowercase

**Today:** At the bottom of every page, under "Signature Finishes," the service names displayed in all lowercase, like "concrete staining" and "epoxy flooring," instead of their normal capitalization.

**After:** They now read correctly as "Concrete Staining," "Concrete Polishing," "Epoxy Flooring," "Decorative Coatings," and "Garage Floor Coatings."

**Why:** A formatting instruction in the site's shared code was forcing every one of those names into lowercase. It has been removed.

---

## 2. The "Schedule a call" link in the footer was easy to miss

**Today:** At the bottom of every page, the "Schedule a call" link looked exactly like the ordinary off-white text links around it, so it did not read as something to click.

**After:** It now appears in the orange brand color and in bold, matching the "Request an Estimate" link a few lines below it.

**Why:** It is one of the main ways someone can reach you from the bottom of a page, and it did not look like a button or a priority. Now it stands out.

---

## 3. Your city pages and questions page were unreachable by clicking

**Today:** You have individual pages for McAllen, Edinburg, Mission, Harlingen, and Brownsville, plus a page answering common questions. None of them could be reached by clicking anything on the website. A visitor could only land on them by finding them in a search result or typing the address directly.

**After:** All six now appear as links at the bottom of every page, so visitors and search engines can find them from anywhere on the site.

**Why:** Pages nobody can click through to are effectively invisible, both to your customers and to Google.

---

## 4. Your Spanish pages showed English menus and forms

**Today:** You have three Spanish pages. The main text on them was in Spanish, but the menu at the top, the footer at the bottom, the bar along the bottom of the screen, and the pop-up quote form were all still in English. A Spanish-speaking visitor saw a half-translated site.

**After:** All of it now appears in Spanish on those pages, including every label in the quote form and the messages a visitor sees after they submit it.

**Why:** A page that switches between two languages looks unfinished and costs you trust with exactly the customers those pages were built for.

---

## 5. There was no way to switch to Spanish

**Today:** Even though the Spanish pages existed, nothing on the English side pointed to them. A Spanish-speaking visitor landing on your homepage had no way to find them.

**After:** A language switch now appears in the top menu on every page. It takes the visitor to the matching Spanish page where one exists, and to the Spanish homepage otherwise. It works the same way in reverse from the Spanish side.

**Why:** Browser settings do not automatically send visitors to a Spanish version. Without a visible switch, the Spanish pages were unreachable for the people who need them.

---

## 6. Two Spanish pages sent visitors to the English contact form

**Today:** The main "Request a quote" button on the Spanish services page, and a similar link on the Spanish home page, both sent visitors to the English contact form.

**After:** Both now go to the Spanish contact page.

**Why:** The Spanish contact page was finished the day before but nothing had been updated to point at it, so Spanish-speaking visitors were being handed an English form at the last step.

---

## 7. The headline on the home page did not say what you do

**Today:** The main headline reads "Built to Last. Designed to Impress." It says nothing about concrete, epoxy, staining, or polishing.

**After:** It now reads "Epoxy, Polished, and Stained Concrete Floors Built to Last." *(Pending final review.)*

**Why:** That headline is one of the strongest signals Google uses to understand what a page is about, and it was not naming a single one of your services. Every other page on your site already names its service in the headline. The home page was the exception.

---

## 8. Search engines had no instructions file

**Today:** Your site was missing a small standard file that tells search engines where to find your list of pages.

**After:** That file has been added.

**Why:** It is a basic piece of setup that helps Google find and revisit all of your pages reliably.

---
