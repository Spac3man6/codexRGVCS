### The single most important finding

**CORRECTED ON REVISION 2. The first draft of this audit published "84.3% of demand is epoxy" as a precise, load-bearing statistic. That number is wrong and is retracted.** What follows is the defensible version. The retraction is explained in full in the revision log at the end of this document, because it is the most important thing a reader of the first draft needs to know.

Google's Business Profile search-terms report **suppresses any term below 15 impressions** and displays it as "under 15". The six named terms sum to exactly 460, which is also the stated total. But roughly 40 further terms sit below the suppression floor and are therefore **not in that 460**. They carry somewhere between 1 and 14 impressions each.

So the real denominator is not 460. It is somewhere between roughly 500 and 1,020.

| Category | Impressions above the reporting floor | Share of the **visible** terms | Share of **true** total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Epoxy-explicit (5 terms) | 388 | 84% | **somewhere between 38% and 78%** |
| Staining-explicit (1 term) | 72 | 16% | unknown, and partly branded |
| Polishing-explicit | not reported | not reported | **not zero. Below the reporting floor** |
| Decorative coatings-explicit | not reported | not reported | **not zero. Below the reporting floor** |

**The "0%" figures in the first draft were the worst error.** Zero was never measured. Google is structurally incapable of reporting a number under 15 in this view. Two recommendations were built on that phantom zero: demoting polishing and decorative coatings in the nav (F-01) and rewriting five city page titles away from "decorative concrete" (F-03). Both are downgraded accordingly.

Three further problems with the metric, all of which survive into the corrected version:

1. **Wrong unit.** Google documents these values as monthly-deduplicated **unique users**, summed across months, not searches. Calling them "what people typed" with exact counts misreads the metric.
2. **An internal inconsistency this audit printed and failed to reconcile.** The same export shows **4,767 profile views against 460 searches**, a ratio of 10.4 to 1. If 460 were the true total of searches surfacing the profile, each would have to produce more than ten profile views. It cannot. That alone should have flagged the denominator.
3. **Circularity.** This metric is demand multiplied by *this profile's existing ranking*. The business has a still-indexed legacy site full of epoxy and garage pages, a "Flooring contractor" GBP category, and a brand name containing "Concrete Stain" that almost certainly inflates the "concrete stain" row with branded lookups. The data may be telling us Google associates this business with epoxy because its indexed footprint is epoxy, not that the market wants epoxy. Acting on it risks a self-fulfilling loop.

**What still holds.** Epoxy and garage phrasing clearly outweigh staining in the visible data, and the phrasing finding is independent of the denominator: people type "epoxy garage floor," not "garage floor coatings." That is enough to justify F-02, the garage-plus-epoxy page rewrite, which needs no percentage to stand up. It is not enough to justify reordering the nav or rewriting five city pages.

**Before acting on any of this, run these five checks. They are free and total about 45 minutes:** count the "under 15" rows in the GBP export; compare the term data month by month rather than aggregated, since April to September is the hottest part of the RGV year and staining is disproportionately exterior work; pull Search Console's query report; run Keyword Planner on the McAllen DMA for polishing, decorative, and Spanish variants, which gives demand independent of this profile's ranking; and check how much of the "concrete stain" row is branded. See F-01 through F-05.

## Section 5: Local, GBP, and citations

Google Business Profile: verified, owner-managed, 51 reviews at 4.5 stars, 4,767 profile views and 460 searches April to September 2026, primary category "Flooring contractor", 74% of discovery on Google Search mobile.

### Weighting note, added on revision. Read before acting on anything in this section.

The first draft of this section tagged the citation findings (F-30, F-31) as High priority on the reasoning that "NAP inconsistency is the single most damaging defect for local ranking." **That reasoning is out of date and has been retracted.** Citation consistency mattered that much roughly 2015 to 2019. Google's entity resolution has since improved to the point that it does not need Yelp to agree with the Business Profile to know they are the same business.

Per [Whitespark's 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors](http://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/), a survey of 47 local search practitioners across 187 factors, local pack weight breaks down as:

| Signal | Local pack weight |
|---|---|
| Google Business Profile | 32% |
| On-page | 19% |
| Reviews | 16% |
| Links | 15% |
| Behavioral | 8% |
| **Citations** | **7%** |
| Personalization | 3% |

Citations are sixth of seven. **Nothing on Yelp, BBB, or Procore is going to move this business in the local pack.** Priorities in this section were rescored accordingly.

Two things follow from this table that the original audit got backwards:

1. **F-32, the GBP "Areas served" field, is the genuinely high-value listing item**, because it is a first-party field inside the 32% category, not a third-party citation inside the 7% category. It is also a ten-minute edit. If the client does exactly one listing task, it is this one.
2. **Citations carry roughly 13% of AI search visibility weight, close to double their local pack weight** ([weighting summary](https://w3marketinghub.com/seo/local-seo-ranking/)). That is the real argument for cleaning up Yelp and BBB: an LLM answering "who does epoxy floors in the Rio Grande Valley" reads those listings directly. It is an AI-visibility play, not a local-pack play, and it should be sold to the client that way.

**Unexamined and larger than citations: link signals at 15%.** This audit did zero backlink analysis. That is a real gap, flagged here rather than hidden.

## Revision 2: red team findings

Three adversarial agents were run against this audit and against the code shipped today. They found more than the audit's own review pass did. Everything below is a correction to, or a gap in, the work above.

### R-01 The link fix shipped today does not work for AI crawlers. Critical

All navigation is injected via `innerHTML`. Verified across all 20 pages: **zero** static `<header>`, `<footer>`, or `<nav>` elements, zero `<noscript>` fallback, and `about.html` contains **zero anchors of any kind** in raw HTML.

Googlebot handles this. It renders, re-parses, and follows the links, at the cost of a render-queue delay. **GPTBot, ClaudeBot, and PerplexityBot do not render JavaScript at all.** To them this site has almost no internal link graph.

For a client served by a practice positioned on AI visibility, this is the worst structural fact in the codebase, and today's work deepened the dependency rather than reducing it. **The "nine orphaned pages fixed" claim in F-09 is true for Googlebot and false for AI crawlers, and it was overstated even for Googlebot:** the five city pages already had 3 static inbound links each. Only `about.html` was genuinely orphaned in static HTML.

**Fix:** hardcode the header and footer as static HTML in all 20 pages, or add a small build step that injects them. This is its own scheduled piece of work, not a quick patch. **It is now the highest-value structural item on this site.**

### R-02 Only two pages of this site are indexed. Critical

Measured: `/` and `/contact` are the only current URLs appearing in search. **Not indexed:** all five city pages, `/faq`, all three Spanish pages, `/services`, and all five service pages. Meanwhile 17 legacy 404s still rank.

This reframes the whole audit. **Sections 3 and 4 of this document optimize schema, meta descriptions, and heading structure on pages Google has not indexed.** Indexation should have been checked first and was not.

Root causes are mechanical: no live `robots.txt`, no static link graph (R-01), and a stale deployed `site.js` (R-03). Whether this is a discovery problem or a quality rejection can only be settled in Search Console's Pages report.

### R-03 The deployed `site.js` is an older build than the repo. Critical

Live is 22,582 bytes, local is 30,498. The deployed file has zero references to `siteCopy`, `cityPages`, `pagesEs`, or `locale`. **Roughly 12 statements in this audit describe code that is not in production.** The HTML is only about 2 days stale; the JavaScript is far behind.

Consequence: live `/es` and `/servicios` render English chrome, and the live footer renders cities as plain text with no links.

**Deployment trap:** local `es.html` and `servicios.html` now point their Spanish CTA at `contacto.html`, which returns 404 live. **Deploy the HTML without `contacto.html` and the Spanish CTA breaks.** Ship the working tree as one unit and verify `/contacto` returns 200 before anything else.

### R-04 The performance section was roughly 2x wrong and named the wrong bottleneck. High

This audit predicted `/epoxy-flooring` and `/garage-floor-coatings` at ~404 KB and called them the heaviest pages. **Real measured transfer is 202 KiB each. Lighthouse mobile Performance is 87 to 90.** Performance is not a problem on those pages, and F-24's premise is wrong.

The error was summing every declared `srcset` candidate and every lazy image, which no browser downloads.

**The real bottleneck, never named in this audit:** 843 ms of render-blocking from the Google Fonts stylesheet, a 1.2 KB file. This document says "Lighthouse" zero times and "Core Web Vitals" zero times outside a heading. **F-27, self-hosting fonts, was rated Medium and should be the top performance item.**

**Also missed:** static assets are served `cache-control: public, max-age=0, must-revalidate`. Every repeat visit revalidates every asset. Add a `headers` block to `vercel.json`.

CrUX field data remains unmeasured; the PageSpeed API returned HTTP 429 on every attempt. Lighthouse lab data is not field data.

### R-05 The audit told the client they have no analytics. They have analytics, and we billed them for it. High

`index.html:89` carries `<script defer src="/_vercel/insights/script.js">`. `memory/seo-log.md` records "2026-06-25, Vercel Analytics setup and troubleshooting, Live, billed and delivered."

The client roadmap said "your website currently has no analytics installed." **That is a billing-integrity problem, not a wording problem.** Corrected: the roadmap now acknowledges Vercel Analytics, explains its limits, and proposes GA4 alongside it.

### R-06 The GBP decline framing in F-05 is not defensible. High

F-05 reports a "42% decline from the June peak" and calls the legacy 404s "consistent with" it. Three problems:

1. **The peak was May (118), not June (117).** May is pre-cutover, when the legacy pages were still live.
2. **The series is 65, 118, 117, 92, 68.** August (68) is within 5% of April (65). That is a two-month spike returning to baseline, not a decline. The audit chose the framing that supported urgency.
3. **There is no mechanism.** GBP interactions are local pack and Maps events. A dead website URL does not remove a profile from the local pack. "Consistent with" implies a mechanism that does not exist.

Better explanations, unexamined: south Texas summer seasonality, and the industry-wide local pack visibility drop reported through mid-2026. There is also an epoxy ad campaign proposal in this repo dated June 2026; nobody checked whether paid media was running during the spike.

**The two-minute diagnostic nobody ran:** compare monthly interactions against monthly views. If views held flat while interactions fell, it is a conversion problem, not a visibility problem. **F-05's causation link should be removed, not hedged.**

### R-07 The redirect map wastes most of what it recovers. High

Google treats a 301 to a non-equivalent page as a soft 404. The proposed map sends **11 URLs into one destination**, which is close to a worked example of the pattern Google discounts.

Worse, it **routes city equity away from the city pages that exist.** Four legacy garage-city URLs (Brownsville, Harlingen, Mission, Edinburg) have live counterparts and the map sends all four to a generic service page, in the same finding that reports those city pages have no visibility.

The map was also written without reading the legacy titles. `/garage-flooring` is titled "Garage Flooring McAllen Tx". `/epoxy-flooring-pharr` is titled "Epoxy Flooring **McAllen**". The legacy site was a McAllen-targeted service-plus-city architecture, not a generic service architecture.

**Rewrite:** map 1:1 where a true equivalent exists; send city URLs to their city pages; leave 404s in place for cities with no page rather than creating soft 404s; and **hold `/epoxy-flooring-pharr`**, the single best-performing legacy asset, until the Pharr page in F-40 exists.

The audit found that none of the five city pages appear in search, then recommended building two more from the same template (F-40) plus contextual links from all five (F-04). It measured that the format produces zero visibility and prescribed more of it. **No finding asks which URL should own which query.**

### R-09 Opportunity cost was never weighed, and the comparison is in this repo. Medium

14 image paths under `/assets/img/city-placeholders/`, 4 containing "temp", 2 "generated", 2 "ai-". **The live homepage LCP element is `home-hero-temp-ai-warehouse-800.webp`.** A flooring contractor whose portfolio is AI-generated is an authenticity risk, the city pages have no genuine local proof, and this contradicts this repo's own standing rule on AI imagery.

### R-11 E-E-A-T is absent. High

No license number, no insurance detail, no years in business, no team names, no owner bio, no social links anywhere on the site. Directory data indicates the business was established in 2009. **Seventeen years of operating history appears nowhere.** For a trade where a homeowner hands over a house key, this is the largest on-page trust gap, and it is gated behind the F-44 client questions.

### R-12 The Spanish vocabulary may be targeting the wrong words. Vic's call

`es.html` uses **"tinción" 14 times against "tinte" once**, and **"garaje" 15 times against "cochera" zero**. Commercial Spanish for this product skews to "tinte ácido" and "concreto teñido"; "tinción" carries a laboratory sense. In northern Mexican and RGV Spanish, "cochera" is the ordinary word for a home garage.

If the vocabulary is off, the proposed `/pisos-epoxicos` and `/pisos-de-garaje` target phrases nobody in the Valley types, and the empty Spanish SERP stays empty. **This is a 20-minute native-speaker check that gates about 7 hours of build work.** Also note F-39's proposed falsification test does not work: GSC was verified 2026-06-25 with no backfill, and the Spanish pages had no internal links until today, so GSC will report near-zero Spanish queries regardless of real demand.
